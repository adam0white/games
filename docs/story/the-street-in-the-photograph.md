# The street in the photograph — proposed continuation

Drafted 2026-10-10; review by 2026-10-12 after owner play. **Not delivered gameplay, established history, or an activated release requirement.** This proposal follows the existing [street-on-the-wall scene](the-street-on-the-wall.md), [history](history.md) and [character boundaries](characters.md). Its photograph, discoveries and sample dialogue below are new authored possibilities, not facts that earlier players secretly witnessed.

## Why return

The new [public solo-maker report](../games/working-life-playtests-2026-10-10.md#public-solo-maker-player--clean-alpha3--home-garage20--hf-220) valued a complete small repair and a useful unfinished step with Dad. It rejected another lamp sequence or a forced move into training and management. Other recorded players lingered over the model's shadow and the family disagreement hidden in reused packaging. These are different preferences with a shared request: another particular thing worth doing.

Give the player an immediately puzzling picture: **the paper street seems to match the photograph until they reach the last shop.** Its roof is familiar, but its doorway is in the wrong place. Is the model wrong, is this another street, or did something change? The player can investigate physically and keep something they have made. A family surprise adds warmth; it does not turn the episode into an explanation of the world's decline.

Target one substantial optional sitting, roughly 12–20 minutes as a design hypothesis, not a wait requirement. Four discoveries and one authored choice should carry the time. A player may stop after any meaningful observation and return without redoing it.

## The invitation and the actual work

After the existing model has been discovered, offer **“Look through the flat sleeve beneath the old fronts.”** This is newly authored storage inside the player's existing box, not an unexplained delivery or a reward for running a business. It contains a creased photograph and its folded protective paper. The first view shows enough to invite comparison; no conversation, repaired drive or completed cabinet is required.

1. **Find the viewpoint.** Set the photograph upright beside the model and move between front and corner views. The low window and two upper windows line up, but the roofline looks much taller from the childhood viewing height. Moving between positions visibly changes which details align. Large named comparison targets and tap controls replace tiny precision dragging. There is no single magic camera angle or hidden-pixel success test.
2. **Follow the wrong doorway.** Lift the existing removable frontage, retaining it beside the model. Compare its pencilled outline with the photograph's fold. Unfold the photograph to reveal a second narrow doorway hidden by the crease. The childhood drawing combined two shops into one. Let the player try a removable paper divider against the model and see how much this changes the little street. This is a visible new construction, not another inspect–repair–wait sequence. Use a newly recorded finite strip from the protective paper; do not pretend an already-spent backing sheet was replenished.
3. **Look at the temporary sign.** The opened photograph shows a printed new-goods sign with a hand-written repairs notice tied below it. A stack of returned crates partly blocks the new doorway. These are evidence of two uses coexisting at the time of the picture. They do not establish a manufacturing ban, official order, exact date, collapse event or villain. The contradictory scene is more interesting than a narrator explaining it away.
4. **Notice the person at the edge.** The newly unfolded edge includes someone holding a display base outside the shop. The player can note a familiar patched coat, but cannot identify the person with certainty alone. If Dad later agrees to look, the proposed surprise is that it was him—and that he was waiting to collect the clearance base, not opening a shop. This connects to the existing provenance of the model's base without granting him ownership of the shop or omniscient knowledge. His reason for keeping the photograph is smaller: he liked how much could fit in one window.

Possible Dad line, for later authorship review: “You gave it a tower. It was the edge of the next roof. I liked your version.” His teasing accepts the child's invention instead of marking it wrong. He can recall carrying the base while remaining unsure when the photograph was taken. Do not make Mum confirm this account or retroactively settle the existing TUESDAY/THURSDAY disagreement.

## A real staying-small choice

Let the player make and keep one of two versions: the remembered single shop, or the photograph's two doors with the new removable divider. Both retain the original frontage and photograph. Switching later is possible because this is arrangement, not destruction. Neither version earns a better repair score.

Then choose where the comparison belongs:

- **Keep it at the private table.** The two versions can be compared in the room light; the photograph remains accessible beside them. Dad, if he participated, notices the chosen version in a later relevant conversation. This is a complete ending with a visible kept object, not the absence of a public reward.
- **Prepare it to show one person.** Write a short question on the retained sleeve: “Do you remember these two doors?” Choose an already-met person to ask on a future ordinary encounter. This creates a specific future conversation, not a customer contract, exhibition business, calendar task or promise of that person's presence. Nothing requires immediately following this route.

The reason to return is the other person's partial answer, or to play with the version kept at home. Avoid an obligatory cliffhanger: the evening resolves what the player made even though the street's history remains incomplete. A small life produces a thing and a memory of its own.

## Availability, continuity and stopping limits

Do the tactile scene alone. “Save this question for Dad” records the actual question without speaking for him; a present, willing Dad can join separately. An interruption preserves the comparison and gives a clear solo continuation. Do not place meals and quiet breaks between each discovery or require repeated requests to finish the scene. Any later shared exchange still needs actual participation; the design is to avoid needing participation for the physical payoff.

An unrepaired model is fully sufficient. Its shutters stay still; no gear, full circuit, competence or repair is awarded. A repaired drive or owned checked bedside lamp may enrich presentation only if actually available. Borrowed samples, absent people and finished cabinet doors do not become decorative props. Retained/rebuilt fronts, spent card, custody away with Nora, and a put-away model keep their real histories. The photo is not accessible while its owning box is away unless it was actually separated beforehand.

Build only this evening if selected. No new light job, stand fault, delivery round, worker role, paid collection or time-passing montage belongs in its length. Before calling it worthwhile, watch a solo maker manipulate the comparison without prompting and a returning player choose to inspect the unfolded edge. Ask what they wanted to find out and whether their final version felt worth keeping. A quick satisfied finish is valid; repeated clicks and elapsed minutes are not substitutes for curiosity.
