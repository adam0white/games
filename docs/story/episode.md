# Episode and disclosure order

## School-break campaign

The first holiday begins at fourteen with one family request. Optional lamp questions reveal the kitchen renovation, then shared meals and Nora's stove. The player's childhood lamp brings the pencil mark and saved drawings. Their first repairs offer no public company controls. Questions disclose memories, not hidden faults or skill awards.

The garage is shared space. Bench marks lead to the unfinished cabinet; Mum's old-room answer allows her book and quiet practice. During school, Sam can discuss a future afternoon together while remaining a friend with other obligations. Arranged observation and whole-job teaching become playable after the player graduates; an invitation does not establish that learning has already happened. No paid apprenticeship or business ownership is established during school.

School transitions explicitly summarize the unplayed weeks and months. The current authored pacing uses two careful returns before a return-to-school opportunity, repeated across four years; this is a playable pacing choice, not a claim that each summer contains exactly two repairs or two afternoons. Names, objects and notes remain recognizable when another summer starts. Household members also have lives between episodes, without silently manufacturing player expertise.

Suggested/current disclosure stages:

| Stage | Ordinary detail | Larger implication |
|---|---|---|
| Fourteen, opening holiday | Kept lamp, donor parts, labelled shelf | Repair is normal family practice. |
| Fifteen, next summer | KEEP IN SERVICE boxes with incompatible standards | An optimistic scheme was abandoned before completion. |
| Sixteen | School poster and Nora's changed main street | Institutions still promise a future their surroundings do not fully deliver. |
| Seventeen | Sam repeatedly unpacking returned pumps | Maintenance is sustained work, despite old promises. |
| Eighteen, school complete | Exercise books beside the repair notebook; garage terms | Work and learning now need an adult agreement. |

The phase-gated questions and `schoolStoryBeat` provide authored material. The engine owns whether a transition occurs, actual elapsed time, who is present and what has been agreed. Original optional question ids remain single-use. Unselected memories remain unasked.

## Working chapter

After school completion, Dad and the player can agree use of the garage; a small shop remains another possible later location. Nora asks permission for a referral. Sam discusses what the work would involve, hours and payment. The first apprentice is found and taught here, not automatically awarded by finishing high school.

The next meaningful transition is another person owning intake, diagnosis, repair, verification and a customer outcome without the player's expertise. That still requires the MVP's real HF acquisition controls. The growing workshop should retain the personal lamp, saved drawings, bench notebook, unfinished cabinet and early customer histories. A drawing or household project can become worthwhile hands-on work while someone else carries a supported repair.

## Later world

Repair's prevalence supports the economy and multiplayer ambition. Scrapping, recovering parts, refurbishment and eventually other businesses can expose different sides of the same material history. Those systems are future scope. Do not expose dashboards, invented economic facts or multiplayer promises merely because their story basis exists.
