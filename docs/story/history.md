# History and world facts

## Before the opening

1. The player grew up in this house and still lives here. Their childhood room is now a spare room used for ironing, reading and quiet prayer. Their present bedroom is across the landing. The old bedside lamp remains in the small room. Its pencil line marked clearance over model roofs, not a move away from home.
2. In a winter before the opening, kitchen renovation displaced meals into the front room. Mum acquired the reading lamp then. Dad's three-day estimate was optimistic. Nora lent a camping stove; Dad cooked eggs outside in his coat. The kitchen became usable; Friday meals in the front room continued for a while.
3. Mum helped Nora with curtains that same year. Neither keeps a debt ledger of these favours.
4. Dad's bench has a plant-pot ring and a glue/clamp mark. The split-shade donor lamp hung above paint tins. Dad retained it partly for its good switch. Taking a sound holder does not make every donor part usable.
5. The kitchen cabinet functions without its two doors. Hinge drawings are in Dad's notebook. The unfinished doors do not contradict the kitchen being usable. School transitions preserve the actual cabinet stage; they do not move, hang or finish a door offscreen. A completed cabinet remains a shared accomplishment in later memories.
6. Before its first intake, Sam’s torch was already unreliable when he dropped it on the path, as he reports in that episode. This report belongs to that episode's date, not every later year. Its missing strap was replaced with red parcel string. The drop is history, not proof of its hidden fault.

## Broad world facts

- High school is the end of formal education. There is no college. People continue learning in work and life.
- The setting is slightly dystopic and repair-dependent. There is much scrap and many abandoned intentions. The owner names WALL-E as inspiration, without borrowing its named characters or exact world history.
- New objects and useful consumables have not ceased to exist. Scarcity is uneven. Do not make batteries free/infinite or claim an absolute manufacturing ban without a later explicit decision.
- Standardization schemes left incompatible older parts despite optimistic labels. Some main-street shops changed from new-goods sales to repair or storage. A maintenance-free promise on a pump did not eliminate maintenance.
- Institutions, geography, ecological causes, ownership of scrap and dates of decline remain undecided. No character has omniscient knowledge of those unknowns.

## Present events depend on the run

Repaired, checked, shelved, returned, taught, hired and paid are distinct outcomes. Do not narrate a later episode as if every player completed the same optional repair. School-year transitions preserve labelled objects, received evidence, people and actual accomplishments; they do not silently complete unfinished work.

Nora’s kitchen lamp and Sam’s torch are the first neighbour requests. Later catalogue entries carry their own provenance, including Nora’s pantry lamp and Dad’s salvaged inspection lamp; teaching lamps are separate objects. A revisited sewing box retains its original identity and the earlier hinge repair when a later catch problem appears. Object history must not be recycled into an inexhaustible anecdote. Historical engine-3 exports retain adult-visit wording as provenance; that wording is not current canon.

## Objects carried forward

Mum’s kitchen timer has an accessible obstruction, not a failed enclosed spring. Dad keeps his bicycle bell after passing an incompatible older frame to someone with the right wheel. Nora’s sewing box carries blue cloth left from her curtains; smaller patches expose gradual material scarcity. Sam’s school timetable remains folded beneath his first parts-counter rota. Nora’s later pantry inventory has become a button drawer, and Dad saves numbered depot returns because the original jobs may still need their parts. These histories do not establish the cause of the wider scarcity.

The player’s paper-street model combines childhood paper shopfronts with a clearance windup base. Its compatible gear is missing; winding it harder cannot repair that. A later donor may make it move again. The printed “A BETTER HIGH STREET” intention remains an ordinary piece of old planning, not proof of a secret scheme.
