# First-minute teaching — 2026-11-05

The human playtester’s cold-side and meal-break questions are addressed through an ordinary staffing decision. The player opens a real cook plan, chooses a station, a 20-minute break and cover, then asks the cooks. The station/break/cover picture changes with the proposal. Refusal remains possible and leaves the ordinary staffing consequence visible. The service lesson waits for the actual requested cook’s reply. The money picture connects meals to the Saturday bill and the actual failure rules: an unpaid bill adds a strike and carries the shortfall forward with 10% added. Two strikes or an unpaid final-week bill closes the kitchen. Broken promises can also add a strike. No simulation rules or difficulty settings change.

One attached prompt appears at a time. Skip is available from the first step. Game menu → Replay kitchen practice restarts the guide in the current game. Before starting or between services, it points to the next ordinary continuation control. Closing an editor or cook panel returns to a visible control. Replay preserves the live game and unresolved choices.

## Synthetic skim test

One gpt-6.1-terra tester at low effort used fresh Chromium contexts, screenshots and control labels. The instruction was to skim and not read body text. These are its exact reaction words, recorded after the actions; this is not a human playtest or a live transcript.

Initial words:

> “There are a lot of words. I'll skip practice and open the doors.”

> “These are requests to the cook, and the possible reply tells me he may refuse.”

It found that replay before starting had no visible prompt. That was corrected. Independent design review also found missing recovery after closing panels and an early result prompt before a request had resolved. Those paths were corrected. Hosted review then found that menu replay discarded an unsubmitted board draft, that the money picture could retain old values, and that opening the doors before the walkthrough hid the service guide. Replay now keeps the current board DOM and its draft. Money pictures update from live host state, and the guide follows the actual service phase even if the player bypasses the board lesson.

Same-tester retest words:

> “A plan is only a request. Dilek said no, so my cover isn't settled.”

The tester made a real station/break/cover proposal, reopened the editor without losing its draft, observed a refused cover proposal and a refused service request, and checked menu replay and skip. It did not observe a complete loss or complete a campaign. The tester misread the currency sign as pounds; the game uses Turkish lira (₺). The earlier diagram also incorrectly implied that the first unpaid bill closed the kitchen immediately. Hosted review caught that error; the corrected diagram follows the strike, carry and final-week rules above. That wording correction has not received another synthetic retest.

## Automated first-minute check

`npm run test:ui -- tutorial.browser.ts --workers=1` scripts the ordinary opening using only control selectors and attributes. It never reads body text. It changes the real staffing plan, asks the crew, makes a service request, waits for its answer and checks replay/skip. Separate interruption checks cover closing the editor, closing the cook panel, replay before starting, preserving an unsubmitted draft and opening service directly. A dev-only host fixture verifies that the money picture follows a changed live till; it does not claim native earnings or replay from that fixture. On the combined HF 4.17 build, all 13 checks passed in Chromium and all 13 passed in WebKit. This includes the four-size controls and a separate regression that a new kitchen resets an active replayed lesson after an earlier skip. An earlier HF 4.12 WebKit run failed once during native game startup, before the board appeared; its seed was not captured and the cause remains unknown. Failure reports now attach the seed. The clean HF 4.17 run does not establish what caused that earlier failure. Viewports: 360×690, 1280×800, 820×1180 and 1180×820.

The first check failed before teaching existed. The new checks exercise the real game UI and preserve the simulation’s own inputs, decisions and consequences. Automated controls do not prove human understanding.
