> **What this is.** An independent design written from scratch on 2026-11-25 by a different model (Codex, `gpt-6.2-astra`) given only the genre, our constraints and no access to the Cinderhill spec. It is kept as a source of ideas and traps; 09-round2-findings.md says what we took. It is not our game. Its names (including a carrier called Burr, which clashes with our beetle) are its own. Links in it were cited by that model and not all were re-opened by us.

# The Weight of Clover

*A cooperative ant-colony RTS for one or two people in a browser.*

## 1. A nest on both banks

A clover head rolls into a crack in a drying meadow. One bank holds enough seed to feed a generation. The other holds a queen and twelve hungry larvae. Sixteen of your forty-eight workers can span the crack by locking their feet together. That leaves thirty-two to carry seed, dig, and tend the brood. Then the first seed comes home, so heavy that two ants have to walk backward. You can watch it cross on the backs of its sisters. You can also see the queue forming behind it.

You lead a colony of **stitchfoot ants**, a fictional meadow species whose workers can hold a span indefinitely while it is anchored at both ends. Your main strategic resource is the number of ants you can spare to stand still. A large colony can alter the shape of its territory. A well-built nest can release hundreds of ants back into circulation. The satisfaction we want is the instant an excavated passage opens, the last carrier clears the old bridge, and the bridge itself walks home.

The colony lives in **Tallow Fold**, a meadow growing on deep, cracked clay. Over the course of three scenarios a strip of sod pulls apart, tips on its side, and settles into two separate islands. The player sees this from ant height in a top-down plan. A bent clover stalk marks the same place across all three maps. What began as a food patch becomes a landmark across open water. The campaign ends when both islands can raise brood independently.

Two individuals make that change personal. **Burr**, the first carrier, has one pale foot. Tap her in the opening scene and the camera follows her first round trip. She obeys ordinary worker rules. **Pip**, the first daughter queen, begins as a pale brood case occupying a nursery slot. Near the end the case splits and the small queen walks the route you built for her. We tell this story through work you can interrupt, inspect, and finish. A player who skips every line of text still sees who crossed.

## 2. Running the colony

### What a player does

Scout an exposed bank, assign workers to a crossing, bring back food, then use the proceeds to change the nest. An order persists until it finishes or the player recalls it. While the workers execute it, inspect the next seam in the clay. Its width, the food beyond it, and the height of its banks are visible before committing. Scouts reveal the contents under plant cover. They never conceal the existence of a required crossing.

In the opener an existing nursery has room for all twelve larvae. Tap Feed and choose a brood count. Each larva takes two portions and recruits one local idle nurse for a growth cycle, then emerges as an adult. Start with four portions in storage. A clover head yields twenty-four more, so spending every portion on growth leaves a healthy colony with an empty pantry. Adults can survive a lean spell. Brood development waits for the next delivery. This gives a novice time to inspect a stalled colony while an experienced player can start digging with a deliberately small workforce.

Sixteen workers extend the first bridge from the near lip until the leading eight grip the far bank. On release, eight step onto each bank. Recall names a home destination and previews a dry return path. Without one, released crews stay on their bank. Transfer moves free workers across an open route. It opens one lane. The second lane takes eight additional workers and passes traffic in the opposite direction. On a single lane, a batch of up to four loaded carriers has priority, then a waiting outbound batch gets a turn. The ants at each end visibly turn around to admit the next batch. Widening the bridge with only two carriers waiting usually wastes labor. Widening it during brood evacuation can save a nursery. The inspector shows recent crossings per minute and ants waiting at each end.

Base building changes that calculation. Drag a digging order across contiguous clay cells, then assign a use to the excavated floor. A **pantry** provides eight portions of capacity per cell and a closer place to unload. A **nursery** holds two larvae per cell and needs a dry approach. A **gallery** carries two-way traffic below ground and can pass beneath shallow cracks. A clay sill on the terrain view shows where a gallery can cross. Deep cracks need a bridge. Excavation consumes worker time. Fitting a pantry or nursery costs two portions per cell, representing food for the fitting crew. The build preview shows the food remaining after reservation. Cancel before work starts for a full release. A finished room can be emptied and reassigned at the normal fitting cost.

Try the following position. A pantry is full, a carrier is waiting on the east lip, and eight free workers could widen the bridge. Inspect the carrier. Her destination reads “waiting for pantry space.” Another lane will deliver her to the same full room. Feed two larvae to free four spaces immediately, fit a second pantry for future deliveries, or open a gallery to unused storage on the next bank. Each solution leaves a visibly different nest. This is the kind of problem a second player should be able to diagnose from across the table.

### Threat, recovery, and victory

The fictional **pleat mite** hunts by vibration. Four consecutive loaded crossings on a marked root wake it. Its pleated back unfolds one segment per crossing, visible even with sound off. When fully awake it pins the next arriving carrier for a ten-second struggle. Assign eight free workers to drive it back before the struggle ends and the carrier lives. Otherwise the mite takes her and retreats. A bypass avoids exciting the root. Sending a guard detail costs workers for as long as you keep it there. A mite encounter makes traffic scheduling matter without asking anyone to tap a moving enemy.

| Scenario | Decision under pressure | End condition |
|---|---|---|
| **One Clover Wide** | Commit workers to the temporary span, then choose between a wider bridge and digging underneath it. There is enough food for either opening. | Raise twelve new adults and release every bridge worker onto dry ground. The player chooses when to end the scenario. |
| **The Fold Turns** | Rain enters the low gallery first. Excavate a shorter rescue route or use the slower dry ridge while the mite stirs on its root. | Carry at least eight of twelve marked brood cases to the upper nursery before the third pool fills. |
| **Two Shadows at Noon** | Prepare the second bank for Pip. Keeping an expensive bridge through the final split gives late carriers time. Closing early puts more workers to work on the new bank. | At separation each bank must contain a queen, four brood cases, eight food portions and sixteen free adults. |

Water advances along printed elevation bands at announced simulation ticks. The inspector gives the next arrival time in seconds. Water closes a tile to new entrants. Ants already on it get one marked retreat interval to reach adjacent dry ground. Stranded brood is lost when that interval expires. A bridge crew holds until traffic has drained unless a player explicitly orders emergency withdrawal and accepts the displayed losses. After failure, show the last affected delivery with its route highlighted. Retry from the start of that rain phase or replay the entire seed. Each scenario supplies its own starting colony. The finale opens with 3,072 workers and full seed heads along both banks. A settled queen lays one egg per vacant nursery slot each growth cycle. Eggs become larvae the following cycle. Food, nurses and floor space support growth to the 4,096-worker cap.

### One thumb, thousands of ants

A tap on the meadow inspects. A tap on an anchor offers **Span** if a second anchor is within reach. Tap the far anchor to see a dotted line, crew count and change in free labor. A fixed bottom button reading “Assign 16” commits it. The same pattern handles a pantry, excavation, guard post, or evacuation. Once two destinations are possible, the player can tap either endpoint in the order card to replace it. A draft never advances on its own. The colony keeps moving behind it.

The bottom edge holds three task cards, ordered by the player's pins, with the remaining orders in a scrollable list. Each card gives the verb, actual crew, requested crew, and the reason for any deficit. Tap plus or minus for one worker. Hold to step by eight, then sixty-four. Half and All assign a share of the available crew in one tap. The allocator draws from reachable idle workers first and shows their arrival time. A transfer from another order previews the donor, the quantity, and the consequences. Workers finish a loaded trip before transferring. Brood nurses and occupied bridge crews require an explicit release. This is how one thumb diverts a few hundred ants while keeping a vital crossing open.

A single drag on bare ground pans. Dig mode supports either painting or tapping the first and last cell of a previewed path. Zoom buttons and “surface / burrow” sit beside the map. Pinch, mouse wheel and keyboard shortcuts duplicate these actions. Keyboard users can move a cell cursor with arrows, inspect with Enter and cancel with Escape. A location list exposes named anchors, rooms and threats as focusable HTML buttons. Keep targets at least 48 CSS pixels across, give traffic a direction pattern, and use icons and words for load, danger and crew shortage. Reduced motion replaces the crawling overview with stationary direction marks and live counts. Sound starts after interaction.

At close range a carrier shifts her grip and another takes the rear of the seed. At meadow scale a thick broken line shows how much is moving and each gap reveals the direction of travel. A tap at either scale returns the same census. The game can represent 4,096 workers across both banks. Every worker occupies exactly one place in the labor ledger. The camera can show fewer bodies on a small screen. It cannot alter an order, a waiting time or a casualty.

## 3. What players have actually said

Two players described the same ant behavior in opposite terms. In a Steam discussion of the reference game, one player read workers turning back as disobedience. Another liked seeing traffic that recalled watching ants as a child. The developer explained the workers' periodic returns. Our answer is to keep the return trip and let the player tap a worker to see her load, destination and next assignment. In a playtest, ask the owner why one carrier passed a new dig site. If the answer requires reading our rules aloud, change the inspector. [Firsthand Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/463530/discussions/0/1489992713711741464/)

MeriStation's reviewer of *Empire of the Ants* appreciated quick troop assignments and variety across missions, and wanted more mechanical depth. That is one critic's assessment of a different ant RTS. Here, the thumb performs the same assignment in every scenario. What changes is the opportunity cost. Taking eight workers off a pantry queue is affordable at low water. Taking eight off a rescue detail just before a flood is a different decision. The player can predict both from the map. [MeriStation review](https://as.com/meristation/analisis/analisis-de-empire-of-the-ants-la-estrategia-en-tiempo-real-desde-otro-punto-de-vista-n/)

We suspect some players will spend a happy minute following Burr even when nothing requires attention, then welcome a hard logistical choice. Others may prefer combat or open-ended construction. These accounts establish individual reactions. Their prevalence is unknown. Observe whether the owner voluntarily follows a carrier after learning the controls, then whether they replay to try the other crossing. Those are separate tests of pleasure in observation and pleasure in strategy.

A third player's review describes a predator cutting the food supply needed to replace losses and the cost of repeating a long mission. We keep the possibility of losing a carrier. The unfolding mite, dry bypass and rain-phase retry give the player a decision before the loss and a short experiment afterward. Ask someone who failed to point to the first moment they would play differently. [a player review](https://steamcommunity.com/id/umbrael/recommended/463530/)

## 4. One colony, two seats

Solo begins in the browser with the opening board already active. Save at each phase boundary and after orders in IndexedDB, with periodic progress saves during play. “Invite a friend” pauses at a completed tick, uploads a versioned snapshot, and opens the same colony as a private room. Validate the snapshot's shape, sizes, map version and ledger before creating the room. There is no ranked result tied to imported saves. The transfer has a persistent request ID. Repeating it returns the original room. After a timeout, resolve the request ID before resuming. Offline play forks the local save so later recovery preserves both branches. After success, the local save points to the room and offers an explicitly separate solo copy.

Both seats see both banks and can issue every ordinary order. A small footprint with the other player's pattern shows the object they are inspecting. It confers no lock. A guest can run the nursery while the creator works the surface, then switch simply by tapping underground. A ping puts a short-lived ring on a named location. The simulation runs with either seat present. Restarting a phase requires agreement from both present players, and creator approval whenever the guest requests a restart.

**Authority and traffic.** One Cloudflare Durable Object owns a room. It advances ten integer ticks per second while a seat is active. Each 200 ms it commits two ticks together and sends a delta with a monotonically increasing revision. Input carries seat, command ID, object revision, verb and bounded arguments. The server derives identity from the connection, validates the revision of the touched objects, and orders accepted inputs by receipt. Independent orders on separate banks can both commit. If two people spend the same last workers, the later order gets the current free count and a fresh preview. Only committed work appears solid on either screen. The originating client draws the dotted preview immediately.

The server moves at most 512 worker groups, with exact integer counts and cargo. It merges groups sharing a route, load and progress bin. A bounded pool reserves enough entries for outstanding deliveries and bridge releases. If a new discretionary assignment would exceed the pool, queue it visibly until a group merges. Art uses a separate random seed for footsteps. Send counts, timestamped edge entries and exits, speed changes, room edits and threat events. Clients derive progress between events. Clients animate between the last two committed views with a 200 ms interpolation buffer. A sleeping phone fetches a full snapshot; it never tries to animate every missed step.

| Operating target | Test condition |
|---|---|
| Initial scene interactive within 2.5 s, at most 250 KiB compressed startup transfer | Cold cache, 1.5 Mbit/s down, 100 ms round trip. Measure from navigation to a working Assign control. |
| Typical deltas average at most 2 KiB, or 10 KiB/s per seat at 5 Hz | Measure payload bytes over a full two-seat final scenario. Full join snapshot budget is 96 KiB. |
| Acknowledged input p95 under 450 ms | 120 ms round trip, both players issuing orders. Show a pending state through stalls. |
| Simulate the two-tick batch in under 8 ms p95; draw within 33 ms p95 | Maximum colony, 96 × 64 cells per layer. Measure server work and client frames separately, including a real midrange Android phone and an iPhone. |

For recovery, persist each command with its application tick, within-tick ordinal, result and the committed tick watermark in one SQLite transaction per batch before acknowledging or broadcasting. Snapshot every 100 ticks with a ruleset ID, PRNG state and last applied command sequence. Recover by replaying the committed tail to its watermark. Empty ticks matter to rain and mites. Retain the current and previous verified checkpoints plus phase-start checkpoints for retries. A phase retry creates a new revision epoch so a delayed order from the previous timeline cannot execute. Persist each seat's last processed command sequence and result across compaction. Allow one outstanding command per seat, batching rapid slider changes into the next preview. Reconnection resubmits the same ID safely.

A room link contains a random 128-bit invitation in its fragment. The client exchanges it by same-origin POST for a seat credential in a Secure, HttpOnly, SameSite cookie. Verify Origin on upgrades. The original seat has a separate credential. A third browser sees “Both seats occupied.” Disconnected seats keep their claim for two minutes, then a guest slot reopens. Use server-side expiry and bounded message sizes. Limit accepted commands to eight per seat per second, and coalesce presence updates. Keep invitation secrets out of telemetry. A lease renewed every five seconds by visible clients expires after fifteen. When both leases lapse, checkpoint, cancel the tick timer and freeze game time. A reconnect resumes at the committed tick. Set a storage alarm to delete dormant rooms after seven days and show that expiry in the invite panel.

## 5. Implementation, art and prior art

Serve HTML, CSS, JavaScript and the base terrain atlas through Workers Static Assets. Use asset-first delivery for the game and explicit Worker routes for room creation, joins and WebSocket upgrades. Bind a SQLite-backed room class with a Wrangler migration using `new_sqlite_classes`. Use the hibernation WebSocket API and store seat references in socket attachments. A running timer keeps a room resident. Stopping it and background heartbeats on pause makes idle rooms eligible to hibernate. Preserve previous hashed assets and pin each live room to a supported ruleset through its retention period. Generate binding types with `wrangler types` and pin a tested compatibility date at scaffold. Budget by occupied room-hour. At Cloudflare's published 128 MB allocation, one continuously running room consumes about 450 GB-s per hour. Its five commits per second also mean at least 18,000 row writes per hour if each commit updates one watermark row. Measure the actual write amplification, request usage and active duration in a bounded preview. Log sampled aggregate tick cost, delta bytes and reconnect counts. A free art pipeline says nothing about the cost of running public rooms. Keep solo playable from static assets when admission to new rooms is closed. Use TypeScript with a pure simulation package that accepts a seed, a state and ordered commands. Run it from a browser worker in solo, inside the Durable Object in co-op, and from a command-line replay runner. Fixed-point positions and stable tie-breaking by cell index make written counterexamples portable. Keep each scenario in a text grid with a small typed table of events and objectives. Use HTML for controls, Canvas 2D for terrain and cached sprite stamps, and Web Audio for generated foot scrapes, hollow seed taps and the low click of releasing a bridge. Build the base atlas from our own drawing functions. A designer can change the bend of a leg or the profile of a seed in a code review. [TypeScript licence, Apache-2.0](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/TypeScript/main/LICENSE.txt)

For the first owner play, provide two selectable art treatments of the same position. **Pressed clover** uses cream clay, dark olive ants and flat wine-red seed coats. **Wet clay** uses umber banks, pale worker markings and white reflections around a loaded span. Both share geometry, hazard hatching, hit areas and UI. Start with Pressed clover for clearer silhouettes. Let the owner choose after locating the last loaded carrier on the smaller screen. Use system fonts and original generated assets throughout. Keep generator source and licence notices with the build.

## 6. Ordered playable milestones

Each milestone ends with the owner playing a specific position from a browser link. Record the scenario seed, his decision and the observed result with the build. A passing replay can establish correctness. the owner decides whether the choice itself earned another round.

1. **Cross one crack.** Ship a static solo board with forty-eight workers, two anchors, one clover head and a nest. the owner assigns sixteen, follows one seed home, then releases the span onto both banks. Accept when he can point to who held the bridge and who carried the seed without opening help. Count all forty-eight after recall. Deliberately remove the far anchor and verify that Assign explains the missing connection.
2. **Build a way home.** Add galleries, pantries, brood and the complete first scenario. the owner solves it twice, once by widening and once by tunneling. Accept when both openings can win, when he can explain the difference in their queues, and when refreshing midway resumes a viable colony. Reconcile every seed portion against food on the plant, carried, stored, reserved or consumed.
3. **Share the first bank.** Put the opening scenario on a deployed two-seat room. the owner invites a friend from a running solo game. Both spend the same workers, disconnect the creator, and let the guest land the next seed. Interrupt a commit and repeat a command after reconnect. Accept when both screens match one ledger and neither an extra worker nor a duplicated spend appears. Measure real room-hour usage here.
4. **Rescue the brood.** Add the mite, rising water and the second scenario. the owner deliberately loses the lower nursery and tries a different route from the phase checkpoint. Accept when he predicts the next flooded approach from the map and saves eight cases on retry. Reapply an old-epoch order in a test. The new timeline must reject it.
5. **Separate the banks.** Add Pip and the finale, including a full-size colony fixture. the owner and a friend choose when to release the last bridge in portrait and desktop layouts. Verify each bank's inventory independently at separation. Accept when a loss shows exactly which bank lacked what, a win shows Pip occupying the second nursery, and both real phones meet the frame and input budgets.
6. **Open the invitation.** Stage the candidate in an isolated namespace, admit a bounded play group and exercise an old client across a release. Test a full room, expired link, slow join, paused room and restored previous build. Verify stopped tick writes while idle and compare observed usage with the intended hosting budget before increasing admission. the owner accepts release after completing an unprompted cold start, invite, background/resume and final delivery from the public candidate.

## 7. Ten traps

1. **The bridge is always worth building.** Put a shallow sill beside a rich patch and a deep crack beside a poor one. Compare food delivered per free worker. Adjust distances until an early bridge and an early gallery each win a different position.
2. **A thriving colony looks like a stalled one.** Render the carried seed at every useful zoom. Give the oldest waiting load an inspectable reason. Play the full-pantry position silently and ask which change will move that load.
3. **The player can strand half the nest by accident.** Ordinary recall first closes admission, then drains occupants. Quote exact losses on emergency withdrawal. Test a carrier stepping onto the bridge on the recall tick.
4. **The phone demands precision under pressure.** Snap a tap to nearby named features, show alternatives where targets overlap, and commit at the bottom edge. Complete a rescue holding the phone in one hand.
5. **Workers appear out of nowhere at high zoom.** Conserve integer membership and cargo on every split, merge, death and transfer. Compare ledgers across camera positions. A representative sprite can disappear behind a rock while her load remains inspectable.
6. **Two people quietly undo each other's work.** Show the partner's pending location, reserve against committed revisions, and identify rejected orders by the resource that changed. Play the simultaneous last-eight-workers case over an impaired connection.
7. **A reconnect becomes free food or lost progress.** Persist command receipts with the watermark. Inject a disconnect after storage commits but before delivery of the acknowledgement. The repeated request must return the original result.
8. **More ants make the phone hot without changing play.** Cap cosmetic draws, cache terrain, and aggregate distant lanes. Measure a full finale including thermal slowdown. Reduce cosmetic density before changing capacities or routes.
9. **An idle tab consumes the room budget.** Expire visibility leases on the server and cancel timers. In preview, abandon two phones mid-session, then inspect duration and writes. Return to the same waterline.
10. **Agent-built content passes tests and feels interchangeable.** Give each map one decision that its predecessor could not pose. Have the owner demonstrate that decision from the play link. If widening the first bridge solves all three levels, revise the terrain before adding another species.
