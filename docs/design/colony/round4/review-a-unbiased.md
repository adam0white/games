## Overall verdict

**Assumed. Build the first three chapters before committing to all six.** I would expect a stranger who likes swarm games to try Cinderhill and finish its opening. I can see that player staying for 20 minutes if the first rain makes them reconsider a tunnel they dug and the spider makes them reconsider a trail they depend on. I would not yet predict that they come back. The spec gives a precise account of what the player sees in five minutes. It gives a much weaker account of what the player might want to try differently tomorrow. The best reason to return is already in the design: the player planted something, it changed where the ants could travel, and that particular hill is theirs. (docs/02-spec.md §§2, 4, 6.4–6.6, 8.1–8.3; docs/04-roadmap.md, “Milestones,” R1 through Depth check.)

**Checked.** The owner’s strongest session was the 5,000-ant sandbox. It lasted 1,035 seconds, or 17 minutes 15 seconds. The owner issued 81 orders, averaging one every `1,035 / 81 = 12.8 seconds`, and the colony made `2,122 × 60 / 1,035 = 123` deliveries per minute. In seven campaign sessions the owner saw no birth and no threat. The longest lasted 427 seconds. The 2,000-ant sandbox session lasted only 73 seconds. **Assumed.** The long session justifies taking free play seriously. It cannot tell us whether scale, responsiveness, freedom from quotas, or some combination held the owner. These are repeat sessions from one person. None reached 20 minutes. (evidence.md, “Replays of the owner’s own sessions” and “Facts about the old build.”)

## What is strong

**Checked.** The first delivery is physically plausible now. The old walk speed was `24 × 20 / 256 = 1.875` cells per second. The proposed speed is `45 × 20 / 256 = 3.516`. For the example route, ten cells out, ten back and three down take `23 / 3.516 + 0.5 = 7.04 seconds`, including pickup. Even at twelve cells out, the estimate is `27 / 3.516 + 0.5 = 8.18 seconds`. That leaves nearly two seconds for recruitment and turning before the ten-second gate. The old ordinary brood cycle took `(3,000 + 3 × 1,200 + 4,800) / 20 = 570 seconds`. The rebuild separates an early emergence from an existing pupa and a later birth funded by the player. **Assumed.** Keep that distinction visible. Seeing an ant emerge establishes that the colony is alive. Recognizing that it grew because I routed the beetle establishes why I should issue another order. (code/sim/sim.js, `TICK_HZ` and `WALK`; code/sim/grid.js, `SUB`; code/sim/colony.js, brood timing constants; docs/02-spec.md §§3.1–3.2, 4.)

**Checked.** Cinderhill clears the project’s design rule on the evidence supplied. The reference connects nursery assignments to numbered pheromone groups, develops its persistent colony through rewards from separate missions, and values nest tiles through adjacency. Cinderhill lets me draw the route itself. A seed from that work can grow cover over a later route. A rival can follow my successful trail back to my door. All three consequences belong to the same place. **Assumed.** That is a distinct game a stranger could describe after playing. The biology bank already supplies enough material through seed dispersal, trail reinforcement and traffic. Adding an unusual species would do less for Cinderhill’s identity than letting the player recognize a route they made possible two chapters ago. (docs/01-reference-research.md §§1–2, 7; docs/02-spec.md §§1–2, 6.2, 6.4–6.6; docs/07-biology-ideas.md §§1.9, 2.2, 2.9.)

**Checked.** The roadmap’s best test gives the owner two chapter-three saves with equal ants and stores on different hills. The owner must change plans and name the reason. The route card’s loads per minute and wait reason can supply visible evidence for that judgment. **Assumed.** Keep this test even if every timing gate passes. It asks whether the shape of a hill changes how someone plays, which is the promised value of free digging, traffic and persistent terrain. (docs/04-roadmap.md, “Milestones,” Depth check; docs/02-spec.md §§6.2, 6.5, 7.)

## Blockers

### 1. The acceptance rules cannot all apply as written

**Checked.** R0 expects the current build to fail the pace gate. Each later R1 step is a separate pull request, and feature 1 says CI fails a pull request that breaks any line of §3.1. The first implementation step can fix delivery and birth timing. It cannot yet pass bird, chapter or human-input gates. The table also requires a threat telegraph by 60 seconds, while chapter two’s first specified rain warning comes at 120 seconds. The single opening schedule and the per-chapter contract imply different tests. R1 also calls for a real-phone stress fixture containing rain and a rival, while the roadmap introduces water at R2a and the rival at R2b. (docs/02-spec.md §§0, 3.1–3.2, 8.1; docs/03-features.md, R1 features 1, 6, 12; docs/04-roadmap.md, “Milestones” and “R1 in build order”; docs/05-tech.md, “Rebuild plan,” Stress fixture.)

**Assumed.** Give each gate a chapter, an actor, and the first milestone where failure blocks a merge. R0 can pass by detecting the old opening and the deliberate slowdown. Once a replacement line passes, protect it. Measure R1’s actual swarm on the phone, then repeat with working water and again with the working rival. Decide explicitly whether chapter two needs an earlier threat or whether its two-minute rain warning is intentional. A builder should never need to insert a warning solely to satisfy an unrelated opening test. (docs/02-spec.md §§3.1, 8.1; docs/04-roadmap.md, “Milestones,” R0 through R2b; docs/05-tech.md, “Rebuild plan.”)

### 2. Chapter three has no settled economic decision or result for losing it

**Checked.** The dusk bill asks the player to choose between spending a load on growth and storing it for hardship. Adults consume sugar automatically. The queen lays free eggs while sugar is positive. Ordinary larvae consume protein, and majors consume three protein. The bill accepts seeds or sugar. Choosing which route to staff could create a real tradeoff, but the spec supplies no bill rate, seed conversion rate, or worked example showing when another worker helps or hurts. It also says a shortfall kills weak ants at the chapter card, while chapter three ends when the bill is paid and the spider is dealt with. A player who drives off the spider one seed short has two plausible outcomes. (docs/02-spec.md §§3.2, 6.1, 6.3, 8.1–8.2; docs/03-features.md, R2 features 18–20.)

**Assumed.** Before building the chapter, specify one dusk with two viable plans from the same save. Give the starting population, remaining time, yields, upkeep, bill, and survivors under each plan. My recommendation is to let a shortfall cost adults and advance if the colony still meets the survival conditions. Show the projected deficit when the player can still redirect a crew. Then the choice can be concrete: keep the beetle route feeding future majors, or move those workers onto seeds before dusk. That example would give the builder both an economic target and an unambiguous chapter transition. (docs/02-spec.md §§6.1–6.3, 7, 8.1–8.2; docs/04-roadmap.md, Depth check.)

## Major findings

### 3. Chapter one scripts events that good management can prevent

**Checked.** The median bot stays on Steady. The scripted jam calls for Surge on the long third route. The starting door admits `3 × 6 = 18` ants per second **in each direction**. Four hundred adults do not imply 18 arrivals per second. For illustration, 100 foragers on a 30-cell route could sustain at most `100 / (60 / 3.5 + 0.5) = 5.7` return trips per second before porch travel. A synchronized departure could still cause a queue. The total colony size alone cannot predict ten queued ants for five seconds. The nursery has a similar problem. With one egg per second and 60 seconds of development, a continuously fed colony settles at roughly `1 × 60 = 60` occupied brood cells. Its nursery holds 80. A full nursery at minute three needs another cause. (docs/02-spec.md §§3.1–3.2, 4, 6.2–6.3; docs/03-features.md, R1 feature 5; docs/04-roadmap.md, R1 Chapter 1.)

**Assumed.** Make both lessons conditional. Let the player who creates a queue widen the door and watch loads per minute recover. Let someone who already widened it see their investment pay off when traffic rises. Test congestion separately with a defined route, assigned crew and release pattern. Give a well-fed colony something useful to build even if its nursery never fills. If competent play averts a problem, that should be a successful outcome the simulation can recognize. (docs/02-spec.md §§4, 6.2–6.3, 7; docs/04-roadmap.md, “How we know it works.”)

### 4. The first bird demonstrates danger more clearly than it demonstrates a choice

**Checked.** Both first-husk placements are free. The stalk placement also happens automatically if the player ignores the prompt. The bird takes at most `5 / 300 = 1.67%` of the starting adults per pass. One husk shelters `3 × 3 × 4 = 36` ants. Recall sends ants to the nearest shelter in §3.2 and sends every outside ant home in §6.7. Those outcomes differ precisely when the player has placed the husk well. The script allows the visible consequence as late as 75 seconds, while the gate requires it by 60. (docs/02-spec.md §§3.1–3.2, 4, 6.7; docs/03-features.md, R1 feature 6.)

**Assumed.** Keep the bird gentle. Make the consequence legible in work performed: the covered crew keeps hauling, a recalled crew retains its loads but misses deliveries, and an exposed crew risks losses. Choose one Recall destination rule and show which ants reached the husk. Ask a fresh player which route stayed productive and why. Purposeful placement by four of five testers is valuable evidence about the gesture. Their ability to predict the second pass would be evidence that they learned the strategy. (docs/02-spec.md §§4, 6.2, 7; docs/04-roadmap.md, R1 Chapter 1.)

### 5. A decision every 30 seconds can coexist with very little player agency

**Checked.** A newly seen source, an empty source, and a full room all count as decision events. The finale lists six beats, including dusk, night and dawn. Even at the six-minute minimum, there are `360 / 30 = 12` half-minute intervals to cover. The spec calls for fuller scripts before implementation, so the skeleton is unfinished. The missing detail is which decisions can produce different outcomes. Chapter one itself ends at dusk and permits doing nothing. A player can reach its card without demonstrating much understanding. (docs/02-spec.md §§3.1, 4, 8.1; docs/04-roadmap.md, “How we know it works.”)

**Assumed.** For each chapter, describe one decision a capable player could reasonably make either way. In the finale, plugging a tunnel before the flood could preserve the nursery while denying guards a short route to the raid. Keeping it open could make sense on a hill with a second dry nursery. Both plans use systems already promised. That single encounter would tell a builder more about the intended finale than a longer list of scheduled alerts. Let a player enjoy watching a plan work between decisions. (docs/02-spec.md §§6.2–6.5, 8.1; docs/04-roadmap.md, Depth check.)

### 6. The release can pass without evidence of a voluntary return

**Checked.** At the stated chapter lengths, chapter three starts about `4–6 + 6–12 = 10–18` minutes into a median-bot run. Rain and the spider therefore matter more to the 20-minute question than another refinement of the opening. Yet outside players are required at R1 and release, while the intervening depth check belongs to the owner. If 60% of starters begin chapter two and 30% of that group finish Spring, `0.60 × 0.30 = 18%` of starters finish. The recommendation question reaches chapter-card viewers and reports earlier departures separately. None of these measures records someone choosing to reopen their hill. (docs/02-spec.md §§0, 3.1, 8.1–8.3; docs/04-roadmap.md, “Milestones.”)

**Assumed.** At R2a, let fresh players stop whenever they want. Record how many reach 20 active minutes, which mode they choose, and how many reopen the game on a later day without a reminder. Use all starters as the denominator. Keep the bloom picture on the card and show one concrete consequence that will persist into the next chapter, such as a dry route grown from their first midden. That gives a returning player something specific to recognize. (docs/02-spec.md §§2, 5, 6.6, 8.3; evidence.md, sandbox rows; docs/04-roadmap.md, R2a and Depth check.)

## Minor finding

**Checked.** Recovery offers any 20-second checkpoint in the previous two minutes, which spans `120 / 20 = 6` intervals. The storage contract names the current checkpoint, previous good checkpoint, chapter start and a command suffix. It does not say how long the history needed to reconstruct every offered retry survives. **Assumed.** Test selecting the oldest offered retry, issuing a different first order, closing the tab and reopening that branch. This matters most after a flood, when a player may need to undo the tunnel placement that caused the loss. (docs/02-spec.md §8.2; docs/03-features.md, R1 feature 9; docs/05-tech.md, “Decisions,” Saves.)
