# Characters

All people are fictional. Their voices and biographies must not be derived from the owner's family.

## You

Fourteen at the first school holiday; eighteen after four authored school-year transitions. Lives with Mum and Dad. Moved from the small childhood bedroom to a bigger room across the landing before the opening. Drew model buildings under the bedside lamp and marked possessions in pencil. Current repair understanding comes from actual play and teaching. No engineering degree, adult career, college, spouse or children. No gender established. Graduation permits a conversation about regular work, not a claim of expert competence.

## Mum

Reads in the front room and sometimes the small former bedroom, which also holds ironing. Warm, lightly teasing, direct when she wants time to herself. Kept selected childhood drawings because the player asked. Enjoys having the player around in the holidays without being permanently available to help. Ordinary prayer is part of her private life; she does not explain a religious ruling or promise supernatural repair outcomes. Nora is a longstanding friend. Tea is company, not a transaction. School preparation, her own reading and household tasks remain real interests.

## Dad

Makes and repairs household things at the garage bench. Stores recoverable parts, records some work in a notebook, and has unfinished kitchen cabinet doors. Practical humour and toleration of untidy progress; neither infallible mentor nor incompetent comic foil. His bench stays shared. Holiday tinkering does not authorize a commercial intake desk. After graduation he can discuss a larger workshop arrangement while retaining his own space. He remembers attempted standardization through the parts it left behind, rather than possessing the explanation for the whole world.

## Nora

Longstanding family friend from before the kitchen renovation. Lent the camping stove; Mum helped her with curtains that year. Her household includes others whose identities are unestablished. Likes a quiet early cup before they wake. Can suggest customers but asks before sharing the player's name. Wants clear prices and the ability to refuse a favour without damaging a friendship. Remembers shops when they sold new goods; does not nostalgically claim everything used to work perfectly. The cake tin circulates between households; do not repeatedly surrender the same tin.

## Sam

Fifteen at the opening, a school friend one year ahead of the player. Has a female dog, an unreliable pocket torch and a spare. Helps pack orders at a parts counter on some holiday mornings; this is not a full-time career or an apprenticeship contract. School and agreed counter hours restrict availability. Red parcel string on the torch is deliberately useful. Curious about diagnosis and willing to arrange an afternoon. A dog-walk commitment takes about fifty simulated minutes; absence governs speech and collection.

After the player's graduation Sam is nineteen, has already finished high school and is taking more counter hours. He may prefer understanding objects to wrapping them, but can refuse a proposed job. Paid workshop learning requires an agreed role, hours and payment. Neither an invitation nor affection automatically transfers expertise.

## Leila

Nora can introduce her with the player's permission. Her porch, landing and gate lights serve ordinary household needs; they are separate objects, not an endlessly returning lamp. During the trial week she plans to return a borrowed trolley to the shared store. A borrowed light is an alternative if the repair cannot fit that time. Hearing her request is not accepting it, and the player can offer a later collection or decline. No family, employer or wider biography is established.

## Evan

Leila can separately offer an introduction to her friend Evan. A salvage-counter contact card also identifies him with display stands and clockwork bases, making the personal model a plausible reason to contact him without a customer referral. These are two possible encounters with the same person, not two invented prior friendships. He uses a folding work light and has an incomplete winding timer. He can bring an old shop-display base after an explicit invitation. Its loose gear may suit the player's model, but he retains its spring housing and labelled screws. Comparing measurements and agreeing an exchange precede taking the part. Business ownership, age and a broader history remain unestablished.

Availability belongs to the engine and HF state. These descriptions guide motives and offers; they do not override observed refusal, fatigue, private obligations or acquired knowledge. Authored calendar transitions summarize life between played episodes; they must not claim unearned repair experience or wages.
