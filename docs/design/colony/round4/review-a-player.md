**Checked** means the supplied files state the claim or supply the numbers for the arithmetic. **Assumed** marks a player response, design judgment, proposed test, or simulation input I supplied. The economy tables are worked scenarios, not forecasts for an unbuilt game. I changed no files.

## 1. The colony and RTS veteran

**Major. Spring has a promising strategic decision, but its depth check can pass even if one plan always wins.** **Checked.** The best reason to play Cinderhill after learning the controls is the shared trail. A productive route can lead a rival scout to the food and then back to your door. The player can keep harvesting and defend, sacrifice throughput to mislead the scout, or use the rival’s trail to raid its granary. Both colonies use the same verbs. This gives a veteran something to learn without borrowing the reference game’s nursery-assigned groups, tile adjacency, species progression, or missions. The roadmap asks the owner to play two chapter 3 saves and change crews or structures for a reason. It does not require the original plan to perform worse on the second hill. The rival arrives two chapters after that check. [02-spec.md §§1, 6.4, 8.1](../02-spec.md), [04-roadmap.md, “Milestones,” Depth check and R2b](../04-roadmap.md), [01-reference-research.md §§2, 6, 9](../01-reference-research.md).

**Assumed.** Keep the two saves and play both plans on both. On one hill, protecting the busiest route should preserve enough income to pay the night bill. On the other, cutting that route should save enough ants or keep a second source open long enough to do better. Record the bill, casualties and deliveries. Repeat the comparison in chapter 5 with a real scout. The veteran’s question is “What did I see that made yesterday’s winning order wrong?” Two different successful orders are weaker evidence than a reversal the player predicted. [02-spec.md §§6.1, 6.4, 8.1](../02-spec.md), [04-roadmap.md, “Milestones,” Depth check](../04-roadmap.md).

**Major. Hour three depends on modes with little specified strategic purpose.** **Checked.** Spring lasts 45 to 60 minutes. A player who finishes it once has **120 to 135 minutes** to spend elsewhere before reaching three hours. Free hill offers population, weather and rival settings. Daily hill scores “loads home plus bloom” without specifying the unit or weight of bloom. The strongest evidence for open play is one owner session with 5,000 ants, 81 orders and 2,122 deliveries in 1,035 seconds. That is 17.25 minutes and 4.7 orders per minute. It is evidence that the owner sustained their own activity. It gives no hour-three result. [02-spec.md §§0, 8.3, 10](../02-spec.md), [evidence.md, “Replays of the owner’s own sessions,” Sandbox](evidence.md).

**Assumed.** Make the daily score the first repeatable test of depth. On one seed, compare harvesting the nearest pile against investing in a sprout that opens a protected return route. Show deliveries and bloom separately before combining them. If harvesting wins both measures on every seed, changing the score weights cannot rescue planting. This is a better reason to revise the hills than a request for more enemies or another season. [02-spec.md §§6.6, 8.3](../02-spec.md), [01-reference-research.md §7, “Replayability / dominant strategy”](../01-reference-research.md).

**Major. The combat test needs equal investment as well as equal headcount.** **Checked.** Three protein buys one major or three workers. The major has four worker-equivalents of health, three of damage and costs two workers’ sugar upkeep. Three workers have three equivalents of health, three of nominal damage and three workers’ upkeep. Only the workers can be in three places at once. The technical test compares terrain at equal headcount. The recovery rule also makes an enemy that has not moved for 20 seconds wander and eventually leave, without exempting an enemy successfully holding a door. [02-spec.md §§3.2, 6.3–6.5](../02-spec.md), [05-tech.md, “Rebuild plan,” Combat](../05-tech.md).

**Assumed.** Test equal-protein forces on an open raid and at a defended entrance. Include income from workers left foraging. Keep an engaged guard stationary for longer than 20 seconds and verify that the rival still holds the door. Otherwise the veteran may discover that waiting beats recruiting, flanking and fighting. [02-spec.md §§6.2–6.5](../02-spec.md), [07-biology-ideas.md §§1.6, 2.4](../07-biology-ideas.md).

## 2. The two-minute phone player on a bus

**Major. The new opening fits the ride. Its first stopping point does not.** **Checked.** Six of seven previous colony sessions lasted at most two minutes. The owner drew a trail within two or three seconds, then waited 73 seconds for food or never saw it arrive. None showed a birth or threat. The rebuild attacks the delay directly. At the representative ten-cell distance, the first load takes `(10 + 10 + 3) / 3.5 + 0.5 = 7.07 seconds`, leaving 2.93 seconds for dispatch and queues under the ten-second gate. A birth is due by 15 seconds, a player-funded birth around 65, and a sprout by two minutes. At precisely that point the script introduces a new pile. The first chapter card is still two to four minutes away, and the daily lasts five minutes. [evidence.md, “Replays” and “Facts about the old build”](evidence.md), [02-spec.md §§3.1–3.2, 4, 8.3](../02-spec.md).

**Assumed.** Let the sprout serve as the first short-session payoff. A rider can leave after making shelter and come back to use it. Specify what happens when the browser hides mid-stroke or during a bird warning. Tactical pause exists, but a rider answering a call may never press it. Twenty seconds of permitted rollback is `20 / 120 = 16.7%` of this visit. The important return test is whether the player recognizes what survived and can make the next decision. Background the phone during the first shadow, reopen it, and check the threat clock, any unintended order, the saved husk placement, and the player’s first action. Report interrupted visits alongside chapter completions. [02-spec.md §§0, 3.2, 4, 6.2, 6.7](../02-spec.md), [05-tech.md, “Decisions,” Saves](../05-tech.md), [04-roadmap.md, “Milestones,” R1](../04-roadmap.md).

**Blocker for the chapter 3 phone build. The decoy lacks an executable gesture.** **Checked.** Trail and Alarm are separate modes. The stroke previews until lift, a second touch cancels it, and a decoy requires Alarm held at the end of a trail drawn to nothing. The spec gives no transition by which one thumb can finish that stroke and hold Alarm on its endpoint. The median bot answers threats with Recall, so a passing bot run need never demonstrate the decoy. [02-spec.md §§3.1, 6.4, 6.7, 8.1](../02-spec.md).

**Assumed.** Put “Make decoy” on the card for a completed empty trail. This uses an existing one-thumb interaction, gives the player time to inspect the trail and makes the issued order observable. Acceptance is a fresh phone player deliberately causing the spider to follow the empty trail. An Alarm animation alone proves too little. [02-spec.md §§6.2, 6.4, 6.7](../02-spec.md), [03-features.md, R2 feature 20](../03-features.md).

## 3. The parent playing with a ten-year-old

**Major. The first bird can teach the wrong lesson even when the child follows the prompt.** **Checked.** A husk provides `3 × 3 × 4 = 36` shelter places. The worked Steady crew has 20 ants. The bird takes at most five ants, or `5 / 300 = 1.67%` of the starting colony. If another exposed ant remains in range on each strike, good and poor placement can both show five deaths. The loss counter lasts five seconds. Section 3.2 sends recalled ants to the nearest shelter, including a husk. The controls table sends every outside ant home. The script permits the first strike outcome as late as 75 seconds, while R1 requires the child’s equivalent of that outcome to be visible inside a minute. [02-spec.md §§3.1–3.2, 4, 6.7, 7](../02-spec.md), [03-features.md, R1 feature 6](../03-features.md).

**Assumed.** Use nearest shelter for Recall. Author the first pass so two otherwise identical replays, differing only in husk placement, visibly diverge by 60 seconds. Show the sheltered crew continuing its route or the exposed crew losing carriers. Then give the child the second bird pass and let the parent stay silent. Can the child point to the crew at risk and predict what moving the trail will do? That tests learning more directly than deliberate husk placement or reaching dusk, which occurs even for a passive colony. Spring supports this on a shared screen with tactical pause. Two-device co-op comes later. [02-spec.md §§3.1, 4, 6.2, 8.3, 9](../02-spec.md), [04-roadmap.md, “Milestones,” R1 and R3](../04-roadmap.md).

**Major. The first flood needs one rescue the pair can understand and complete.** **Checked.** Rain gives 30 seconds’ warning. Ten seconds in a flooded cell kills brood. Construction takes 5 to 20 seconds. The chapter skeleton lists the first flooded tunnel before the plug or mound offer. If the first visible indication that construction matters is water reaching occupied cells, a 20-second job finishes at least `20 − 10 = 10 seconds` too late, even before travel. The spec does not say that the first pulse reaches the nursery. That distinction is what the authored scene must settle. Gentle also converts a loss into a poor score, but the next playable state after losing the queen or all brood is unspecified. The floor supplies adults and sugar. [02-spec.md §§3.2, 6.3–6.5, 8.1–8.2](../02-spec.md).

**Assumed.** Flood an empty low tunnel first, show the higher dry room, and let the child predict where the nurses will take brood before the lethal pulse. On Gentle, show which queen, brood and stores carry forward after failure. A family can accept a poor score and still need to understand how its colony survived. The seed-to-shade loop and brood rescue already offer two constructive roles for a parent and child. They do not require the deferred field guide, tournaments or additional species. [02-spec.md §§5, 6.3, 6.6, 8.2, 10](../02-spec.md), [07-biology-ideas.md §4, “Non-violent and co-op angles for families”](../07-biology-ideas.md).

## 4. The strategy-game economy designer

**Blocker. Section 3 constrains the economy but does not determine either chapter ledger.** **Checked.** Chapter 1 specifies 80 sugar, 60 protein and a later 300-load mixed pile. It omits the mixed split and quantities for the nectar stalk, cricket and later carcass. Chapter 3 inherits its colony, but has no reference start, source schedule, spider loss rate, seed conversion rate or bill coefficient. Section 6.1 charges casualties for a shortfall, while §8.1 requires a paid bill to end the chapter. A literal builder must decide whether an insolvent colony advances, retries or waits at dusk. Those decisions determine which strategy wins. [02-spec.md §§2, 3.2, 4, 6.1, 8.1–8.2](../02-spec.md).

**Assumed simulation method.** I stepped both chapters one second at a time. Each step credits deliveries, charges sugar at `(adults + majors) / 600`, feeds eligible larvae, matures brood, applies scripted losses, then lays into vacant cells. Majors already count as adults, so the extra term charges their double upkeep. Unfed larvae wait and die after 60 seconds. Sugar is shown to one decimal. In both tables, **in** means loads delivered that minute, **spent** means sugar eaten and protein fed, **loss** gives adult and larval deaths separately, and balances are at minute end. These are explicit choices where tick order, supply or mortality is unspecified. [02-spec.md §§3.2, 6.1–6.3](../02-spec.md).

**Chapter 1, five minutes. Assumed.** Start with the specified 300 adults, 60 sugar, 20 protein and 80 nursery cells. Space the 12 starting pupal births four seconds apart from second eight, following the explicit opening schedule. Give the 20 larvae initial ages zero through 19 and distribute the eggs across ages zero through ten. Deliver both opening piles in minute one. Bring 60 nectar in minute two, then 60 sugar and 60 protein from the mixed pile in each of minutes three and four. Make no deliveries in minute five. Leave the cricket untouched, keep the original nursery, and lose five adults on each bird pass. This deliberately tests a player who misses protein, then restores it. [02-spec.md §§3.2, 4](../02-spec.md).

| 1 | 80/60 | 31.4/80 | 32 | 5/0 | 327 | 108.6/0 |
| 2 | 60/0 | 36.7/0 | 60 | 0/5 | 387 | 131.9/0 |

**Assumed result.** The missed cricket is felt a minute late. The colony produces 60 adults in its protein-free second minute from already-fed brood, then only 16 after deliveries resume. By dusk it has `300 + 212 births − 10 adult losses = 502 adults`, above the approximate 450 target despite losing 26 larvae. That delay gives the player a good reason to inspect the nursery. Show the cohort that stopped developing when protein ran out. A total adult counter tells the player things are going well during the minute they made the costly decision. [02-spec.md §§3.1–3.2, 4, 7](../02-spec.md).

**Major. The scripted door jam has no demonstrated cause.** **Checked.** Door capacity is `3 × 6 = 18 ants/s` in each direction. A 20-ant crew cycling every 7.2 seconds sends about `20 / 7.2 = 2.8 ants/s` each way. Even two such crews give only 5.6. For a long route with a 20-second cycle **assumed for illustration**, sustaining 18 arrivals/s requires 360 circulating workers before accounting for other traffic. A larger colony and a longer route do not by themselves produce a queue. Surge can create a departure burst, but the median bot explicitly stays on Steady. Record arrivals, service and queue length for the specified five-second jam on a median replay. Then widen the door and show that throughput rises. If only Surge causes the jam, make it a consequence of that order and let the Steady player finish without it. [02-spec.md §§3.1–3.2, 4, 6.2](../02-spec.md), [03-features.md, R1 feature 5](../03-features.md).

**Chapter 3, six minutes. Assumed.** Begin with 600 ordinary adults, 100 sugar, 80 protein, 80 nursery cells and 60 staggered brood, one per second of development. Credit 100 sugar and 90 protein per minute. Start designating every fifth new egg for major feeding after minute one. Raise laying capacity to two eggs/s after minute two. Keep the nursery at 80 cells. Charge four worker deaths at seconds 90 and 210 for the spider. Apply the bill after the table. These assumptions make the chapter reproducible and expose the value of the queen chamber. [02-spec.md §§3.2, 6.1–6.4, 8.1](../02-spec.md).

**Checked arithmetic on assumed inputs.** Two eggs/s offers 120 eggs/minute, but 80 cells occupied for 60 seconds sustain only 80 births/minute. At one major in five, those 80 births require `64 × 1 + 16 × 3 = 112` protein. Intake of 90 drains reserves by 22 per minute. Meanwhile the sugar balance peaks in minute five, then falls while the colony still appears to thrive. At dusk, a bill of 0.2 sugar per adult costs 202.4 and clears. At 0.25 it costs 253 and misses by 40.4. The same play changes from surplus to default solely through an unwritten coefficient. Specify one reference hill, how seeds convert, when the headcount is sampled, how a shortfall converts to deaths, and the resulting chapter state. Then run a lower-growth plan against it. That comparison would tell the builder whether storing food can actually beat expanding. [02-spec.md §§3.2, 6.1, 8.1–8.2](../02-spec.md), [04-roadmap.md, “Milestones,” R2a and Depth check](../04-roadmap.md).

## Taste calls for the owner

1. **What should a veteran return to master?** Options are the daily score, a persistent free hill, or replaying Spring with a different plan. **Assumed recommendation: a persistent free hill.** The owner’s longest observed session was self-directed, and an enduring rival gives the trail network a strategic history. [evidence.md, “Replays,” Sandbox](evidence.md), [02-spec.md §§6.4, 8.3](../02-spec.md).
2. **What should a two-minute visit feel like?** Options are a finished small achievement, a suspended chapter, or a complete short challenge. **Assumed recommendation: a finished small achievement inside the chapter.** A sprout the rider planted and can use next time is already in the script. [02-spec.md §§4, 6.6, 8.3](../02-spec.md).
3. **What should Gentle preserve after disaster?** Options are the colony before impact, the surviving colony with a visible rescue, or a replacement colony on the same green hill. **Assumed recommendation: the surviving colony with a visible rescue.** It preserves the child’s relationship with this colony while giving the family a consequence it can explain. [02-spec.md §§2, 5, 8.2](../02-spec.md).
4. **How harsh should the first night bill feel?** Options are a warning with no deaths, bounded losses with continuation, or mandatory repayment before advancing. **Assumed recommendation: bounded losses with continuation.** It makes the first reserve decision matter and lets the player try a different policy with the colony they raised. [02-spec.md §§6.1, 8.1–8.2](../02-spec.md).
